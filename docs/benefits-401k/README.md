# Benefits Administration — 401(k) & Annual Enrollment

Experience at **Foundever (Alight account)** as a 401(k) Defined Contribution Specialist, supporting participants of large U.S. employer plans (retail and services sector clients).

## What the work involved

- **Participant support:** contribution changes, balances, loans and withdrawals questions, beneficiary updates, account access.
- **Data accuracy:** input, update and verification of employee benefit data across multiple systems.
- **Sensitive data:** daily handling of U.S. Social Security numbers and financial data under strict privacy and authentication rules.
- **Annual Enrollment:** verifying client/participant data so that benefit elections were accurate and on time inside the enrollment window.
- **Reporting:** clear documentation and reports delivered against strict deadlines.
- **Performance:** exceeded KPIs for call resolution and information processing.

## Key concepts

| Concept | Short explanation |
|---|---|
| **Defined contribution (DC)** | Benefit depends on contributions + investment returns (vs a defined benefit pension) |
| **Pre-tax vs Roth** | Pre-tax lowers taxable income now; Roth is taxed now, qualified withdrawals tax-free |
| **Employer match / vesting** | Employer contributions may vest over a schedule; unvested amounts are forfeited at separation |
| **IRS limits** | Annual elective deferral limit, plus a catch-up limit for participants age 50+ (amounts change yearly) |
| **Loans & hardship withdrawals** | Plan-specific rules; hardship requires a qualifying reason |
| **Annual Enrollment** | Yearly window to elect or change health and other benefits; outside it, only qualifying life events allow changes |
| **Qualifying life event (QLE)** | Marriage, birth/adoption, loss of other coverage, etc. Opens a special enrollment period |

## How this connects to claims work

Benefits administration and claims share the same foundation: **eligibility is the first thing to check**. An enrollment error made during Annual Enrollment becomes an eligibility denial (CARC 27) or a COB problem (CARC 22) months later in the claims queue. Having worked on both ends, I check enrollment and eligibility history early when a claim looks wrong.
