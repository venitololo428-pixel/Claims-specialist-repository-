# HIPAA & Data Privacy in Daily Work

Across every role in my career (health insurance claims, 401(k) administration, retail payments) I have handled protected and sensitive data: PHI, Social Security numbers, card data. These are the practices I follow.

> This is a practitioner's summary, not legal guidance. Each employer's policies and the client's contract define the exact rules.

## What counts as PHI

**Protected Health Information** is any health-related information that can identify a person, for example:
- Name, address, phone, email, date of birth, SSN
- Member ID, claim number, medical record number
- Dates of service, diagnoses (ICD-10), procedures (CPT), provider seen
- Payment and billing information tied to care

## Practices I apply on every contact

| Practice | What it looks like in the queue |
|---|---|
| **Verify before disclosing** | Provider: NPI + TIN + caller name. Member: ID + name + DOB (or the client's required identifiers). No verification, no PHI. |
| **Minimum necessary** | Answer the question asked. A provider asking about claim status gets the status, not the member's full claim history. |
| **Right recipient** | Confirm fax numbers, emails and mailing addresses before sending anything; never send PHI to a personal or unverified contact. |
| **Authorized representatives** | Third parties (family members, attorneys) need an authorization or representative form on file before I discuss the account. |
| **No PHI outside approved systems** | No notes on paper, personal devices, chat apps or screenshots. Case notes live only in the CRM / claims system. |
| **Clean documentation** | Notes record what is needed for the case and nothing more. No opinions, no unnecessary diagnosis detail. |
| **Secure workspace** | Lock the screen when stepping away; follow clean-desk rules; no recording or photographing screens. |
| **Report, don't fix quietly** | A misdirected fax, a wrong-member disclosure or a suspicious caller is reported to the supervisor / privacy team immediately. |

## Related data I have protected

- **SSNs and retirement account data:** 401(k) participant records for large U.S. employers (Foundever / Alight)
- **Payment card data:** card details and membership programs in retail support, under PCI rules (Alorica / Best Buy)
- **Health insurance billing data:** premium payments and account details (First Enroll)

## Social-engineering red flags I watch for

- Caller refuses to verify but "just needs a quick confirmation"
- Pressure or urgency ("the patient is in surgery right now")
- Request to send records to a new, unlisted fax or a personal email
- Caller "already knows" partial data and asks me to confirm the rest
- Requests to change bank or payee information over the phone

The answer in all of these: follow the verification process, offer the official channel, document the attempt, and escalate if needed.
